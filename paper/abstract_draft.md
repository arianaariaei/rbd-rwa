# Abstract (draft, structured)

**Study Objectives:** Automated scoring of REM sleep without atonia (RWA) could find
prodromal synucleinopathy in routine polysomnograms. We asked whether published RWA
cut-offs transfer across recording systems, and what this means for Parkinson's disease
research.

**Methods:** Montreal, SINBAR (chin), AASM and REM Atonia Index criteria were applied by
one open pipeline to 1,177 polysomnograms from three open databases (CAP, including 22 RBD
patients; HMC, n=147; MGH, n=925). Four analyses were pre-specified: a noise ladder;
floor-corrected criteria, subtracting each recording's noise rather than multiplying it;
three further systems (177 nights); and effects on continuous RWA and the open-source
RBDtector.

**Results:** Published cut-offs flagged 29% (Montreal) and 76% (SINBAR) of MGH patients
but 9% and 25% at HMC. Format, amplitude, apnea and age did not explain the gap; raising
MGH's noise floor to HMC's reproduced it, and noisier floors cost RBD sensitivity
(Montreal 90% to 57%). Floor-corrected criteria resisted added noise, closed the gap
(Montreal +20.5 to +1.6 points; SINBAR +51.0 to +2.4) and kept SINBAR sensitivity at 95%.
On quiet new systems, published SINBAR flagged 91% and 84% of healthy volunteers;
floor-corrected, 35% and 32%. Raising RBD nights to HMC's floor lowered tonic RWA by 21
points, two-thirds of the difference that predicted Parkinson's disease (floor-corrected:
1 point); RBDtector flagged 89% of the quietest system's healthy volunteers.

**Conclusions:** Background-relative RWA criteria measure the recording's noise floor as
much as the patient, enough to confound Parkinson's disease research. Thresholds should be
defined against the noise floor, not in multiples of it, and reported with it.
