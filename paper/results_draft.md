# Results (draft)

Numbers are from `results/report.md` (tables T1-T14) and `results/sensitivity_background.csv`,
regenerated by the notebook. Nights with at least 10 minutes of REM sleep; native band.

## 3.1 Recordings

Of 1,253 recordings (CAP 108, MGH 994, HMC 151), one had no chin EMG (CAP n16) and 75 had
less than 10 minutes of REM sleep (CAP 2, MGH 69, HMC 4), leaving 1,177 scored nights
(T1): 105 from CAP (22 RBD, 15 healthy, 40 NFLE, 10 PLM, 9 insomnia, 5 narcolepsy, 3
sleep-disordered breathing, 1 bruxism), 925 from MGH and 147 from HMC. MGH patients were
55 years old on average and 67% were men; median REM duration was 60 min in MGH and 69 min
in HMC.

## 3.2 How often the published cut-offs call a night positive

The Montreal and SINBAR cut-offs recognised almost all CAP RBD patients (91% [72-97] and
95% [78-99]); the RAI recognised about half (55% [35-73]) (Fig. 1, T2). The same cut-offs
flagged many patients without RBD, at rates that depended on the cohort more than on the
method. Among CAP patients with other sleep disorders recorded on the same system, the
Montreal criteria flagged 22% of those with NFLE, 40% with PLM, 22% with insomnia and 60%
with narcolepsy; SINBAR flagged 67-100% of each group. In the two external clinical
cohorts, the Montreal criteria flagged 29% [27-32] of MGH but 9% [5-15] of HMC patients,
SINBAR 76% [73-79] and 25% [19-33], and the RAI 9% [7-11] and 7% [4-13].

## 3.3 The methods disagree on the same night

Agreement between methods was poor wherever positives were common (T3). In MGH the three
methods disagreed on 70% of nights (kappa Montreal-SINBAR 0.20, Montreal-RAI 0.34,
SINBAR-RAI 0.04); in HMC, where few nights were positive, on 22% (0.36, 0.73, 0.29). Even
in CAP RBD they disagreed on 41% of nights, mostly because the RAI missed patients whom
the other two methods flagged.

## 3.4 Sleep apnoea, age and sex in MGH

In MGH, each additional 10 apnoea-hypopnoea events per hour raised the odds of a positive
call by 16-21% (Montreal OR 1.16 [1.05-1.28]; SINBAR 1.19 [1.05-1.35]; RAI 1.21
[1.05-1.39]; T4). Age was not associated with a Montreal or RAI call and weakly with a
SINBAR call (OR 0.89 [0.79-0.99] per decade); men were more often SINBAR-positive (OR 1.43
[1.04-1.97]). Removing REM epochs that contained a scored respiratory event or arousal
lowered the positive share modestly (Montreal 28% to 22%; SINBAR 75% to 68%; RAI
unchanged; T5) and removed the apnoea gradient entirely: in event-free REM the Montreal
positivity was 21%, 20%, 24% and 19% for AHI <5, 5-15, 15-30 and >=30 (T13). MGH patients
without sleep apnoea (AHI <5, n=132) were still flagged at 21-24% by the Montreal and
65-69% by the SINBAR criteria.

## 3.5 The site difference is not produced by amplitude or recording format

The quiet-REM chin EMG level - the reference against which the relative criteria judge
activity - was 2.6 times lower in MGH (median 0.59 uV [IQR 0.47-0.77]) than in HMC (1.52
[1.23-1.79]), and below the 1-uV step in which MGH stores the signal (T9, Fig. 2A). CAP's
RBD patients had a higher level (0.80 [0.61-1.22]) than CAP patients with other disorders
recorded on the same system (medians 0.47-0.60).

Re-recording HMC in MGH's format in software did not reproduce MGH's positivity (T12,
Fig. 2B). Quantising HMC to 1-uV steps and resampling it to 200 Hz left the Montreal
positivity at 9% (SINBAR 25% to 22%; RAI 7% to 7%). Scaling HMC down to MGH's quiet-REM
level (x0.39; median 0.59 uV) left the relative criteria unchanged (Montreal 8%, SINBAR
25%) and lowered the RAI (7% to 3%), as expected for an index built on absolute
amplitude; quantising and resampling the scaled signal, which places HMC exactly where
MGH sits relative to the storage step, changed little (Montreal 8%, SINBAR 20%, RAI 3%).

Residual cardiac artefact did not explain the site difference either: after template
subtraction the heartbeat-locked ratio fell from 1.92 to 1.08 in MGH (p90 5.13 to 1.53),
from 1.26 to 1.01 in HMC and from 1.34 to 1.03 in CAP (T8), and MGH nights with more
residual artefact were not more often positive under the Montreal or RAI criteria (T14).

## 3.6 A higher noise floor turns MGH into HMC

The reverse manipulation reproduced the site difference. Adding 1.5 uV of white noise to
the chin EMG of 300 MGH recordings raised their median quiet-REM level from 0.56 to 1.37
uV (HMC 1.52) and, on the 277 nights with at least 10 minutes of REM, lowered the share
flagged by the Montreal criteria from 34% to 10% (difference -23.5 points [95% CI -28.5 to
-18.5]; 65 nights changed, all from positive to negative; exact McNemar p = 5e-20) and by
SINBAR from 83% to 36% (-47.3 [-53.2 to -41.4]; 131 nights, all positive to negative;
p = 7e-40), close to HMC's 9% and 25% (T12, Fig. 2B). The RAI barely moved (12% to 10%;
p = 0.1). The methods, which disagreed on 70% of MGH nights, disagreed on 28% of the noisy
ones (HMC 22%; kappa Montreal-RAI 0.77, HMC 0.73; T3), and the noisy nights acquired HMC's
floor signature: the ratio of the quietest NREM to the quietest REM chin level fell from
1.20 [1.08-1.43] to 1.08 [1.04-1.15], HMC's value being 1.08 [1.04-1.18] (T16).

Within a cohort the picture differs, because there a night's quiet-REM level also carries
its genuine muscle tone. Nights with a lower level were not flagged more often (T11,
Fig. 5): in MGH a lower level went with fewer Montreal (OR 0.64 [0.55-0.75] per halving,
adjusted for age, sex and AHI) and RAI calls (OR 0.28 [0.21-0.37]) and slightly more
SINBAR calls (OR 1.20 [1.03-1.39]).

## 3.7 The noise ladder: dose-response, both kinds of error, and a system-wide cause

The pre-specified noise ladder (Methods 2.6) turned this single comparison into curves
(Fig. 6). As noise was added, the share of nights flagged fell steadily in every group
without RBD: in MGH from 34% to 23%, 15%, 9%, 8% and 6% (Montreal) and from 83% to 68%,
44%, 29%, 22% and 13% (SINBAR) as the quiet-REM level rose from 0.56 to 2.84 uV. MGH's curve
passed through HMC's own point: read at HMC's median level (1.52 uV), MGH would be flagged
at 8.9% by the Montreal criteria (HMC 8.8%) and 29.2% by SINBAR (HMC 25.2%). After every
night of both cohorts was harmonised to a quiet-REM level of 2.0 uV, the Montreal criteria
flagged 9% [6-13] of MGH and 8% [5-14] of HMC nights (difference +0.8 points, against +25.1
as recorded) and SINBAR 22% [18-28] and 17% [12-24] (+5.5, against +57.9). By the criteria
fixed in advance, the noise floor accounted for the site difference under the Montreal
criteria and for 90% of it under SINBAR, narrowly missing the 5-point threshold.

The same mechanism costs sensitivity. CAP's RBD patients were flagged by the Montreal
criteria in 90% of nights as recorded (quiet-REM level 0.86 uV), 71% at 1.3 uV, 57% at the
HMC-like 1.5 uV and 19% at 3.0 uV; SINBAR held at 95% up to 1.7 uV and fell to 86% at 2.0 uV
and 67% at 3.0 uV; the RAI, which subtracts its own noise estimate, changed least (57% to
43%). Thus a recording system with HMC's noise floor would miss about four in ten RBD
patients under the Montreal criteria that a system with CAP's floor would catch.

The noise difference between the sites is not specific to the chin. The 40-90 Hz floor of a
central EEG channel was 0.16 uV [0.15-0.20] in CAP's main system, 0.23 uV [0.22-0.25] in MGH
and 0.46 uV [0.41-0.82] in HMC - twice MGH's - mirroring the chin floors (0.57, 0.56 and
1.52 uV). Scoring conventions at the edges of REM periods contributed little: without the
first and last epoch of every REM period the MGH-HMC gap narrowed from 20 to 16 points
(Montreal) and did not change for SINBAR (51 to 52) or the RAI (T15).

## 3.8 The definition of background changes the verdict more than any covariate

Re-scoring every night with six definitions of background moved the share of MGH patients
flagged by the Montreal criteria from 13% (median of REM) to 47% (minimum of REM), and by
SINBAR from 45% to 91% (Fig. 3). The ordering of cohorts was the same under every
definition - MGH and CAP's other patients close together, HMC lowest - so the site
difference is not an artefact of the definition. Separation of RBD from other CAP
patients was best with low references (AUC 0.85-0.88 for the phasic measures at the 5th
percentile of REM) and collapsed towards chance as the reference approached the median
(tonic AUC 0.47-0.51).

## 3.9 What this means for screening

Taking sensitivity from CAP RBD and specificity from the pooled external cohorts, the
positive predictive value at an RBD prevalence of 2% would be 7% for the Montreal
criteria, 3% for SINBAR and 11% for the RAI; even at 10% prevalence it would be 28%, 13%
and 41% (T6). The same criteria gave different values at each hospital: at 2% prevalence,
6% (MGH) against 17% (HMC) for the Montreal criteria and 2% against 7% for SINBAR, while
the RAI gave 11% and 13%. Because CAP's patients have established RBD, these values are
upper bounds for a screening population.

## 3.10 Cut-offs re-derived for automated scoring, with and without harmonisation

Re-deriving each cut-off on CAP (Youden's index, 21 RBD patients against 51 patients on
the same system) raised the relative-criteria thresholds (Montreal phasic 15% to 27.7%;
SINBAR "any" 18.2% to 62.9%) and cut the share of external patients flagged: SINBAR "any"
to 10% of MGH and 1% of HMC, Montreal phasic to 8% and 5% (T7, Fig. 4). Leave-one-out
cross-validation retained 81-95% sensitivity at a cost of 0-6 points of specificity on
CAP (T10). The cut-offs were imprecise: across 2,000 bootstrap re-derivations the SINBAR
"any" threshold ranged from 62.9% to 80.4% and the Montreal phasic threshold from 19.7% to
29.3%, with MGH false-positive shares of 3-10% and 7-17%.

Harmonising every night to a quiet-REM level of 2.0 uV before re-deriving the cut-offs did
not make them portable (pre-specified criterion 4, Fig. 7). Harmonisation alone made MGH
and HMC agree under the published cut-offs, but at the cost of sensitivity (Montreal 57%).
Re-deriving cut-offs on the harmonised CAP data to recover it drove them very low (Montreal
phasic 5.4%, tonic 1.5%, SINBAR "any" 13.7%), with leave-one-out sensitivity of 71-90% and
reopened site gaps: MGH 21% vs HMC 10% (Montreal phasic), 40% vs 19% (Montreal tonic) and
30% vs 20% (SINBAR "any"), with wide bootstrap intervals.

## 3.11 Subtracting the noise floor makes the criteria portable

With the noise removed, the three recording systems registered muscle activity at the same
size: the median N2/N3 level was 1.60 uV in MGH, 1.70 uV in HMC and 1.61 uV in CAP's
patients, and the median REM level 0.44, 0.45 and 0.39 uV, although their quiet-REM levels
differed 2.6-fold (0.59, 1.52 and 0.48 uV). The sites differed in noise, not in gain.

The pre-specified floor-corrected (FC) rules (Methods 2.7; B = 1.0 uV) removed the
dependence on noise (Fig. 8A-B). On the same 280 MGH nights, raising the floor to HMC's
1.5 uV lowered positivity under the published Montreal criteria from 33.9% to 11.9% (61
nights changed) but left Montreal-FC at 21.3% (no night changed); SINBAR fell from 83.0% to
33.2% (138 nights), SINBAR-FC moved from 48.7% to 50.5% (5 nights, all towards positive). In
CAP's RBD patients, Montreal sensitivity fell from 90% to 57% with the published rule and
stayed at 81% with FC; SINBAR-FC stayed at 95%. Both FC rules were flat up to a floor of
about 2 uV; beyond it the guard against counting noise takes over and positivity falls,
because small activity can no longer be told apart from noise.

As recorded, FC closed the site gap (Fig. 8C). Montreal-FC flagged 18% [16-21] of MGH and
16% [11-23] of HMC patients (difference +1.6 points, against +20.5 with the published rule),
and SINBAR-FC 45% [42-48] and 43% [35-51] (+2.4, against +51.0). Subtraction mattered: a
clamp that only kept the background from falling below 1.0 uV left gaps of +7.5 and +15.2
points and still lost sensitivity with noise. Against the five criteria fixed in advance,
SINBAR-FC met all five (sensitivity 95%; CAP patients 45% against 75% published).
Montreal-FC met four; its sensitivity, 81% (17 of 21), was below the pre-specified 85%
(published rule on the same nights: 90%). The reference background B set the operating
point without affecting portability: from B = 1.0 to 2.0 uV both FC rules stayed within
2 points along the ladder and within 6 points between sites, while sensitivity and
false-positive rates fell together (Montreal-FC: sensitivity 81% to 57%, MGH 18% to 8%).
With B = 0.75 uV, Montreal-FC kept 95% sensitivity with a 4.5-point gap (exploratory).

FC does not make the published SINBAR chin cut-off specific: at 18.2% it flagged about 45%
of patients at both hospitals. Re-derived on CAP, however, the SINBAR-FC cut-off (49.1% of
3-s mini-epochs) transferred: it flagged 13% [10-17] of MGH and 14% [11-20] of HMC patients
with 86% leave-one-out sensitivity, whereas the cut-off re-derived on the published SINBAR
measure flagged 10% and 1%. Re-derived Montreal-FC cut-offs fell below 8% and did not
transfer (gaps +10 and +12 points). Within CAP, where RBD patients and controls share one
system, FC changed discrimination little (AUC 0.83-0.85 against 0.84-0.85). Like the RAI, FC
depends on gain: scaling HMC by 0.39 lowered SINBAR-FC positivity from 43% to 12% - a
change real sites did not show, since their net muscle levels agreed.

## 3.12 Age

CAP's RBD patients were 71 +/- 7 years old; the same-system patients were mostly younger
(35 under 50, mean 30, mostly NFLE; 16 aged 50 or over, mean 59: PLM, insomnia, NFLE).
Discrimination held against patients of similar age (AUC against patients >= 50: Montreal
tonic 0.87, phasic 0.81, SINBAR any 0.88, RAI 0.81; against all patients 0.84-0.85), but
older patients without RBD were flagged more often: 44% against 29% by the Montreal criteria,
19% against 11% by the RAI, and 62% against 37% by SINBAR-FC (T17, FC9). False-positive
rates estimated from CAP's young controls are therefore optimistic; the external clinical
cohorts (mean age 55 in MGH) are the better guide.
