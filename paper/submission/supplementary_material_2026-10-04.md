Supplement to: The recording system's noise floor drives automated scoring of REM sleep without atonia: four pre-specified analyses of 1,354 open polysomnograms. Numbers are from the pipeline's reports; nights with at least 10 min of REM sleep; published cut-offs unless stated; FC = floor-corrected with B = 1.0 µV.

## S3. Noise ladder (pre-specified, commit 6d3cc6b, 26 September 2026)

**Plan.** Every recording was streamed again and its chin EMG kept as recorded and with white Gaussian noise added in band, after the same 10-100 Hz band-pass and mains notch as the signal. One noise track per night, seeded by record name, was scaled to each level (common random numbers): 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 and 3.0 µV RMS. Two harmonized copies used, per night, the noise that raised the quiet-REM level to 1.5 or 2.0 µV; nights already above the target were left as they were. Cohorts: CAP (every recording with a chin EMG in physical units), HMC (all) and MGH (the first 300 records of the Challenge 2018 list, the same 300 as the earlier noise test). An independent measure, the 40-90 Hz floor of a central EEG channel in N2/N3 and REM, was computed in the same pass. The six criteria in Table 4 of the main text were committed with this plan before any ladder result existed.

## S4. Floor-corrected criteria (pre-specified, commit 20b9ec2, 26 September 2026)

**Derivation.** A relative rule counts activity above k b. When b is noise of RMS n, a muscle signal of RMS a adds to it in power, and the rule is met when √(a² + n²) exceeds kb, that is, for a of at least about √(k² − 1)·b. The muscle activity demanded therefore scales with the system's noise floor: 1.7 b for k = 2 and 3.9 b for k = 4, about 1.0 µV at MGH's floor and 2.6 µV at HMC's for SINBAR. The floor-corrected threshold asks instead for the muscle activity the published rule asks for at a reference background B, whatever the recording's own noise. For band-limited white noise, the 5th percentile of 1-s RMS values is 0.910-0.913 times the RMS at 200, 256 and 512 Hz (simulation), hence n = b/0.91. The guard g = 1.5 was chosen on simulated noise: runs of at least 0.1 s above 1.5 b occurred in 0.4-0.7% of 3-s mini-epochs and never made an epoch tonic (at 1.3 b, 11%). The rule was developed on simulated white noise and checked on three CAP nights only before the plan was committed; the RAI, which subtracts its own noise estimate, was the comparator.

**Limits of the rule.** On CAP's healthy volunteers, recorded on several systems, floor correction turned three nights positive under the Montreal criteria, all on a 128-Hz system with floors of 2.5-3.7 µV. At 128 Hz the 5th percentile of noise RMS is 0.879 (not 0.91) of the RMS and the 1.5 b guard lets noise start 0.1-s runs in 2.3% of 3-s mini-epochs (0.5% at 200 Hz; 8.8% at 100 Hz), and these floors exceed the rule's range (b at most B/0.59 = 1.7 µV for B = 1.0 µV). Floor-corrected scores should be read only at sampling rates of at least 200 Hz, the AASM minimum, and within that range. Three CAP healthy recordings are stored without physical units and were not scored by floor-corrected or absolute rules.

## S5. Recording systems not used so far (pre-specified, commit 48053f3, 27 September 2026)

**Plan.** The plan was committed before any chin EMG of these cohorts was processed; only metadata had been read (licenses, channel lists, sampling rates, units, stage coding and the structure of one DOD-H file). Rules, code paths and parameters were those already fixed. Inclusion was unchanged: chin EMG in physical units sampled at 200 Hz or more, expert or consensus stages in 30-s epochs, and at least 10 min of REM sleep. BOAS (OpenNeuro ds005555, CC0; Micromed SD plus; bipolar chin EMG at 256 Hz; consensus AASM stages of three scorers): 128 nights of 100 adults; each participant's first recording was primary (99 with at least 10 min of REM), all nights a sensitivity analysis. DOD-O (Dreem Open Datasets, Zenodo 15900394, MIT license; Somnomedics Somno HD; 250 Hz): 56 records, one without consensus scoring and two with less than 10 min of REM excluded, leaving 53. DOD-H (same release; Compumedics Siesta; 250 Hz): 25 healthy volunteers aged 18-65. Both Dreem sets provide ECG and consensus stages of five scorers. Mains: 60 Hz for DOD-O, 50 Hz for DOD-H and BOAS. BOAS has no ECG channel: R-peaks were sought on the chin EMG itself with the same detector and plausibility test (35-130 beats/min, more than 90% regular intervals), and the heartbeat was removed only where detection passed (6 of 99 nights).

**Heartbeat check (exploratory, added after the first look at BOAS).** Periodicity of the REM envelope at the heart rate (spectral peak at 0.7-2 Hz over the 0.5-3 Hz median): MGH 2.67 after removal (90th percentile 4.66) and 3.17 with the heartbeat left in; HMC 2.33 and 2.50; DOD-O 2.25 and 2.74; DOD-H 2.67 and 2.61; BOAS 3.07 (90th percentile 5.61). In the 80 BOAS nights no more periodic than MGH's after removal, positivity was the same as in all BOAS nights (Montreal 44%, Montreal-FC 14%, SINBAR 92%, SINBAR-FC 35%). Leaving the heartbeat in, in the cohorts with ECG, changed the published rules by at most 7 points (it raises the background they are multiples of) but raised SINBAR-FC by 9-15 points (MGH 45% to 60%, HMC 43% to 52%, DOD-O 51% to 62%, DOD-H 32% to 40%).

**Results.** Share flagged and the prediction verdicts are in main Tables 3 and 4. BOAS, all 127 nights: Montreal 41% (FC 12%), SINBAR 91% (FC 35%). BOAS first nights by age: below 50 years (n = 74) Montreal 36% (FC 15%), SINBAR 89% (FC 31%); 50 or over (n = 24) Montreal 58% (FC 17%), SINBAR 96% (FC 50%). Net levels (µV): BOAS N2/N3 1.04 \[0.69-2.00\], REM 0.33; DOD-O 1.68 \[0.91-4.04\] and 0.45; DOD-H 1.05 \[0.78-1.97\] and 0.27. Floor signature (ratio of quietest N2/N3 to quietest REM chin level): BOAS 1.20, DOD-O 1.16, DOD-H 1.10. MGH's ladder interpolated at DOD-O's floor (0.82 µV) predicted Montreal 20.9% and SINBAR 61.5%; DOD-O was flagged at 30.2% and 58.5%.

The four narrow misses share an explanation: the two healthy cohorts, recorded on different systems, had the same lower net N2/N3 tone (1.04 and 1.05 µV, against 1.6-1.7 µV in the three patient cohorts), which points to their population rather than to gain; and the re-derived SINBAR-FC cut-off flagged 11-12% of their nights against the 10% or less set in advance, so it is about 88% specific in healthy adults.

## S6. Parkinson's disease analyses (pre-specified, commit 1e7acf0, 2 October 2026)

**Plan.** The plan was written and committed with its code before any RBDtector run. Isolated RBD is the strongest known prodromal marker of Parkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies and multiple system atrophy \[1\], and RWA is used in five ways in that research: diagnosis and trial eligibility; conversion risk (baseline tonic chin RWA 72.9% in later Parkinson's disease against 41.0% in those who stayed disease-free, 62.7% for any neurodegeneration \[19\]); progression (30% to 54% over a mean 5 years in 11 patients \[20\]); an earlier stage, isolated RWA (DREAMER, RAI below 0.85 \[18\]; 25% RBD, 25% isolated RWA and 50% normal in early Parkinson's disease \[21\]); and dementia with Lewy bodies, where polysomnographic RWA is an indicative biomarker \[22\]. No dataset with Parkinson's patients is available without an application, so nothing here is measured in people with Parkinson's disease. The ten predictions are listed in main Table 4.

**RBDtector setup.** RBDtector (commit 7c9c73d, 19 May 2022) was run through its own controller (PSGController.run\_rbd\_detection) with default settings, on input files written in its format: the chin EMG as channel "EMG" in microvolts, band-passed at 10-100 Hz and notched as an acquisition system would (RBDtector does not filter), heartbeat left in, at 256 Hz (CAP decimated from 512 Hz, which is what RBDtector's own spline resampling does at that rate; MGH's 200 Hz resampled by RBDtector); the expert stages as its sleep profile; and no arousals (one dummy event in the first second, which is wake). It ran in the environment its requirements specify (Python 3.9, NumPy 1.21, pandas 1.3, SciPy 1.7, pyEDFlib 0.1.42, the newest release its pin allows, whose renamed sampling-rate key was mapped back to the name RBDtector reads). Two changes made the 1,412 runs fit in Colab sessions without changing any score: a helper that assigns each short REM fragment to the nearest REM block was replaced by the equivalent NumPy operation, and each night was written from two epochs before its first REM epoch to two after its last, keeping the night's clock times so that RBDtector's 30-ms, 3-s and 30-s grids fell where they would. On a test night the scores were identical, to the last digit, to those of the unchanged program on the whole night, and a run took 35 s instead of 81 s. The floor-corrected run of each night reused the baseline its published run had computed from the same files; the ratio of RBDtector's baseline to the RMS of band-limited white noise (0.976) was obtained by simulation.

**Deviations from the run plan (none changed a score).** All 1,412 runs were completed (72 CAP nights at five ladder steps; 100 MGH, 147 HMC and 99 BOAS nights; each with both baselines); no night failed and no ladder step was dropped. The work was split between two Colab runtimes; the second had no Google Drive access, so its record lists were passed to it from the first and its scores file was returned through the browser, with an identical MD5 checksum and exactly the pre-specified MGH and HMC nights. The CAP run stopped once after 61 of 72 nights when a second job exhausted the runtime's memory, and was resumed; scores are written only when a night is complete.
