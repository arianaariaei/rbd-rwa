# Abstract (draft, structured)

**Study Objectives:** Automated scoring of REM sleep without atonia (RWA) could find
prodromal synucleinopathy in routine polysomnograms. We asked whether published
RWA cut-offs, applied automatically, transfer across recording systems, why they might not,
and whether a correction helps.

**Methods:** The Montreal, SINBAR (chin), AASM and REM Atonia Index criteria were implemented
in one open pipeline and applied to 1,177 polysomnograms from three open databases (CAP,
including 22 patients with RBD; HMC, n=147; MGH, n=925). Software re-recordings
isolated storage format, amplitude and noise floor. Three analyses were
pre-specified: a noise ladder; floor-corrected criteria that subtract each recording's noise
instead of multiplying it; and a test on three further systems (177 nights: healthy adults,
clinic patients).

**Results:** Published cut-offs flagged 29% (Montreal) and 76% (SINBAR) of MGH patients but
9% and 25% of HMC patients. Format, amplitude, apnoea and age did not explain the gap;
raising MGH's noise floor to HMC's reproduced it (8.9% vs 8.8%), and noisier floors cost
RBD sensitivity (Montreal 90% to 57%). Floor-corrected criteria were unaffected by added
noise, closed the gap (Montreal +20.5 to +1.6 points; SINBAR +51.0 to +2.4) and kept SINBAR
sensitivity at 95%. On the new systems, the published SINBAR criterion flagged 91% and 84%
of healthy volunteers on quiet systems; floor-corrected, 35% and 32%, and positivity
no longer followed the recording's floor (Spearman rho -0.83 to +0.43).

**Conclusions:** Background-relative RWA criteria measure the recording's noise floor as
much as the patient. Thresholds should be defined against the noise floor, not in
multiples of it, and reported with it.
